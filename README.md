# Adaptive Significance of Amphicarpy as a Bet-hedging Strategy in American Hog-peanut

**Zheng (Neal) Yin** · Honors Thesis in Computational Biology, Brown University · April 2024
Advisor: Daniel M. Weinreich (Weinreich Lab, EEB) · Second reader: Rebecca Kartzinel

📄 **[Read the thesis (PDF)](Neal_Yin_Amphicarpy_Comp_Bio_Honors_Thesis.pdf)**

## Summary
Amphicarpic plants produce two kinds of seeds: large, robust subterranean seeds and smaller, more numerous aerial seeds that can germinate later. This thesis asks whether, and under which conditions, amphicarpy in the American hog-peanut (*Amphicarpaea bracteata*) works as adaptive bet hedging, i.e. trading some short-term mean fitness for lower fitness variance in a fluctuating environment.

- **Model:** stochastic Wright–Fisher simulations of amphicarpy evolution with a seed bank (delayed germination).
- **Parameters:** taken from published field studies and from my own greenhouse experiments on hog-peanut plants (germination and fecundity assays).
- **Findings:** amphicarpy behaves as adaptive bet hedging in variable environments, and the advantage grows with drought severity. The optimal range of aerial-seed germination rates narrows as fitness declines.

**Keywords:** bet hedging · annual plants · mating systems · seed bank · amphicarpy
